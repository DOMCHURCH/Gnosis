Welcome to Gnosis

A few things worth knowing before you start.

What leaves your computer

Gnosis has no servers and collects nothing. There is no analytics, no telemetry, and no account. Your conversations, sessions and files stay on this machine, in ~/.dom and ~/Gnosis.

But it is not an offline app, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. To do its job it sends data to services you configure:

  • OpenRouter — your prompts, and file contents the agent reads. This is the model provider; nothing works without it.
  • Groq — your recorded speech, if you use voice, so it can be turned into text.
  • Brave Search — your search terms, if you use web search.
  • GitHub — a version check, to see whether an update exists.

Those are independent companies with their own privacy policies. We never see any of it — but we cannot make promises on their behalf.

What the agent can do, and who is responsible

Gnosis is an agent, not a chat window. Given the go-ahead it will run shell commands, create and delete files, make network requests, and control your mouse and keyboard. It asks before doing dangerous things, and it can be wrong.

You are responsible for what you ask it to do and for what happens as a result. Gnosis is provided free and as-is under the MIT licence, with no warranty of any kind. Do not point it at anything you cannot afford to lose, and keep backups.

Voice — off by default

Microphone — off

Say “hey jarvis” to talk to the agent, and it answers out loud.

The microphone is off until you switch it on here or in Settings, and nothing listens before then. When it is on, a floating panel shows you it is live — Esc ends a conversation, × turns voice off entirely. Wake-word detection runs locally on your machine; only your recorded speech is sent out, and only for transcription.
